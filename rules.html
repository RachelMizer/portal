0. Materials Needed

To play Portal, each player or table will need:

Shared Materials

  • 1 Shared Portal Deck (150 cards)
  • 1 D-4 Glitch Die
  • 1 Shared Exile Pile area
  • 1 Shared Playmat or table space

Per Player Materials

  • 20 Life tracker (dice, app, or counters)
  • 3 Creature Slot spaces
    • Slot 1 — Creator Slot
    • Slot 2 — Middle
    • Slot 3 — Right
  • Artifact Zone (1–3 artifact spaces)
  • Planeswalker Zone (1 planeswalker space)
  • Personal Exile Pile
  • Mana Pool tracker (dice or counters)
  • Optional: tokens, minions, or counters for cards that create them

1. Overview

Portal is a multiplayer strategy game played with a shared 150-card deck. Players draw from the same deck, exile cards, play creatures into three battlefield slots, cast spells, deploy artifacts, and command planeswalkers while the Portal destabilizes reality through unpredictable Glitch effects.

Portal is fast, dramatic, and chaotic — every Surge reshapes the battlefield.

4. Deck Construction Rules

4.1 Deck Size

  • 150 cards exactly

4.2 Colors

  • Maximum two colors

4.3 Suggested Ratios (Recommendations Only)

  • 50–65% creatures
  • 20–30% spells
  • 10–20% artifacts
  • 5–10% planeswalkers

4.4 Power Level Guidelines

Allowed: Big creatures, splashy spells, token generators, board wipes, buffs, removal, chaotic effects, supportive artifacts, mid-power planeswalkers.

Not allowed: Infinite combos, tutors, cost-reduction engines, extra turns, "you win/lose the game" cards, oppressive lockouts.

4.5 Token-Creator Guidelines

  • 10–15% of creatures may create tokens
  • Tokens should be simple (1/1, 2/2, or temporary)
  • Tokens should not create more tokens

4.6 Bombs

  • 10–15% bombs — dramatic but not game-ending.

4.7 Exile Interaction

  • 5–10% exile-related cards encouraged.

4.8 Shuffle Rules

  • The Portal deck is never reshuffled.
  • Only King/Queen and Glitch effects manipulate its order.

5. Surge Structure

Each Surge consists of four phases:

5.1 Draw Phase

  • Draw 4 cards.
  • Play 1 card.
  • Exile the remaining 3 face-down into personal exile.

5.2 Play Phase

Players may:

  • Play one creature
  • Play one artifact (optional)
  • Play one planeswalker (optional)
  • Play any number of spells
  • Cast instants (only during their own turn)
  • Resolve effects immediately
  • Place creatures into slots (see Section 6)
  • Activate planeswalker abilities
  • Use artifact abilities

5.3 Combat Phase

  1. Declare Attackers
  2. Declare Blockers
  3. Assign Damage
  4. Resolve Damage
  5. Track damage for King or Queen of the Surge

5.4 Upkeep Phase

  • Untap all creatures
  • Determine King or Queen of the Surge
  • Resolve Portal Glitch (starting Surge 4)

6. Creature Slot Rules

Each player has three creature slots:

  • Slot 1 — Creator Slot
  • Slot 2 — Middle
  • Slot 3 — Right

6.1 Creatures never shift automatically

Empty slots stay empty.

6.2 Token-creator creatures force a shift

When a creature that generates a token/minion/copy enters:

It MUST shift into the Creator Slot the first time.
Later token-creators MAY shift into the Creator Slot or enter any empty slot.

If the Creator Slot is occupied, the creature there dies and goes to personal exile.

6.3 New creatures enter any empty slot

Players choose which empty slot to fill.

6.4 If all slots are full

The player must choose a creature to replace. The replaced creature dies and goes to personal exile.

6.5 Tokens/minions/copies

  • Always occupy the same slot as their parent
  • Never use empty slots
  • Disappear if the parent dies

6.6 Slot identity

  • The Creator Slot has special rules
  • Slots 2 and 3 are interchangeable
  • Spells may target specific slots

7. Artifact Rules

Artifacts are non-creature permanents that sit beside your creature slots.

7.1 Artifact Placement

Artifacts do not occupy creature slots. They sit in the Artifact Zone.

7.2 Artifact Behavior

Artifacts:

  • remain on the battlefield
  • may have activated abilities
  • may have passive effects
  • may interact with creatures or planeswalkers
  • may be destroyed by spells or combat damage (if they have toughness)

7.3 Artifact Limits

A player may control up to 3 artifacts at a time. Additional artifacts must replace an existing one.

Return to top

8. Planeswalker Rules

Planeswalkers are powerful allies that sit beside your artifacts.

8.1 Planeswalker Placement

Planeswalkers do not occupy creature slots. They sit in the Planeswalker Zone.

8.2 Loyalty Abilities

Planeswalkers may activate one loyalty ability per Surge.

8.3 Damage to Planeswalkers

Players may attack planeswalkers directly. Damage removes loyalty counters.

8.4 Planeswalker Limits

A player may control one planeswalker at a time. Playing a second replaces the first.

Return to top

9. Creature Death Rules

A creature dies when:

  • Its toughness hits 0
  • It is replaced
  • A spell kills it
  • A Glitch or King/Queen effect replaces it

When a creature dies:

  • It goes to personal exile
  • Its tokens/minions/copies disappear
  • No death triggers occur
  • No ETB/LTB triggers occur unless printed

10. Healer Rules

Healers are support units that exist outside normal gameplay. They provide healing and optional flavor or mechanical bonuses depending on how the deck builder chooses to use them.

10.1 Choosing a Healer

A deck builder may select a Healer in one of two ways:

Flavor Healer — A real Magic card chosen purely for theme. Its printed text is ignored, and standard healing rules apply.

Mechanical Healer — A real Magic card chosen because its printed abilities matter to the deck. The deck builder must ensure these abilities do not create infinite loops or break Portal balance.

10.2 Healer Immunity

The Healer is never treated as a creature. It cannot:

  • attack
  • be attacked
  • be targeted
  • be destroyed
  • enter the battlefield

It functions as a protected support unit outside normal gameplay.

10.3 Healer Position

The Healer never enters the battlefield. It remains next to the Portal for the entire game and must be visible to all players.

10.4 Standard Healing Action

All Healers share the same baseline healing ability:

Sacrifice your Surge (turn): Heal 3 life, or heal a creature you control for 3 toughness.

This is the default healing effect unless a Mechanical Healer explicitly overrides it.

10.5 Placeholder Healer

If a deck builder does not wish to choose a card for flavor or mechanics, they may use a:

  • token
  • counter
  • blank placeholder card

This simply reminds the player that the standard healing action is available.

10.6 One Healer Per Deck

Each deck may designate exactly one Healer. This prevents stacking healing effects or creating multi-Healer loops.

10.7 Healing Timing

Healing resolves immediately when the Surge is sacrificed. It cannot be responded to, countered, or interrupted.

10.8 No Healer Interaction

Healers cannot be:

  • copied
  • cloned
  • flickered
  • bounced
  • exiled
  • recurred
  • targeted
  • destroyed

They exist outside the battlefield and cannot interact with game objects.

10.9 Healer Visibility

The Healer (or placeholder) must remain visible to all players at all times.

11. Spell Behavior

11.1 Sorceries

  • Played during Play Phase
  • Resolve immediately
  • Go to personal exile

11.2 Enchantments

  • Played during Play Phase
  • Resolve immediately
  • Apply effect
  • Go to personal exile

11.3 Instants

  • May only be cast during your own turn
  • May be cast during your combat
  • Resolve immediately
  • Go to personal exile

12. Draw Rules

Portal does not use a traditional hand. Any effect that instructs a player to "draw a card" must follow special handling so the game remains consistent with the one-card-per-Surge structure.

A drawn card is not added to a hand. It becomes a temporary decision object that must be resolved immediately.

Draw Resolution

When a player draws a card in Portal, they must choose one of the following outcomes:

1. Replace the Surge Card

The drawn card may replace the card the player chose as their Surge card this turn.

  • The original Surge card is moved to exile.
  • The drawn card becomes the active Surge card for the turn.
  • The player continues the turn using the new Surge card.

2. Exile the Drawn Card

The drawn card may be placed directly into exile.

  • The original Surge card remains in play or continues resolving normally.
  • The drawn card is not stored, held, or retained.
  • No additional Surge actions are granted.

Important Notes

  • A player may only have one Surge card per turn.
  • Drawn cards never enter a hand.
  • Drawn cards must be resolved immediately using one of the two options above.
  • This rule applies to all draw effects, including ETB triggers, spells, abilities, and replacement effects.

Example: Phyrexian Rager

1. Surge Selection

The player chooses Phyrexian Rager as their Surge card for the turn.

2. Surge Payment

The player pays the Surge cost to play the card.

3. Enter the Battlefield

Phyrexian Rager enters the battlefield.

4. ETB Trigger

Its ability resolves: Draw a card and lose 1 life.

5. Draw Resolution

The player must immediately choose one of the following:

Option A — Replace the Surge Card

  • The drawn card replaces Phyrexian Rager as the Surge card.
  • Phyrexian Rager is moved to exile.
  • The drawn card becomes the active Surge card for the turn.

Option B — Exile the Drawn Card

  • The drawn card is placed directly into exile.
  • Phyrexian Rager remains on the battlefield.
  • No additional Surge actions are granted.

13. King or Queen of the Surge

At the end of each Surge, one player rises above the chaos.

If you'd like to omit this mechanic, doing so will not break the game.

Flavor

The battlefield hums with unstable energy. The Portal pulses, recognizing the strongest presence — the one who struck hardest, or whose creatures tower above the rest. This player becomes the King or Queen of the Surge, gaining momentary dominion over the Portal itself.

Rules

  1. Check combat damage dealt to players
  2. The player who dealt the most damage becomes King or Queen
  3. If no damage was dealt, the player with the highest creature power becomes King or Queen
  4. The King or Queen may choose one card from personal exile and place it anywhere in the Portal deck

14. Portal Glitch (D-4)

Starting Surge 4, the Portal destabilizes reality.

If you'd like to omit this mechanic, doing so will not break the game.

Flavor

The Portal is ancient, volatile, and hungry. Each Surge, its energy surges unpredictably — warping exile, resurrecting forgotten threats, or shuffling fate between players. The Glitch is the Portal's heartbeat, and no one escapes its influence.

Rules

Roll a D-4:

1 — Stable

The Portal calms. Nothing happens.

2 — Creature Recall

  • Reveal the top four cards of shared exile
  • Roll a die equal to number of players
  • The chosen player may replace one of their creatures with one of the revealed cards
  • The replaced creature dies and goes to personal exile

3 — Pass 1 Exile Card Left

  • Each player keeps their left-most exile card
  • Each player passes their right-most exile card to the player on their left
  • Passed cards enter that player's personal exile

4 — Pass 2 Exile Cards Left

  • Each player keeps their left-most exile card
  • Each player passes their two right-most exile cards to the player on their left
  • Passed cards enter that player's personal exile

15. End of Game

The game ends when:

  • Only one player remains above 0 life
  • A Glitch effect eliminates all but one player
  • Players agree to end the session

The last surviving player is the Champion of the Portal.

16. Optional Rules

16.1 Team Play

Teams share strategy but not exile piles, slots, or permanents.

16.2 Boss Mode

One player begins with:

  • +20 life
  • A fourth creature slot
  • Two planeswalker slots
  • A permanent +1 Surge bonus each turn

16.3 Sudden Collapse

At Surge 10, roll the Glitch die twice each Surge.

17. Glossary

  • Surge — A full turn cycle consisting of Draw, Play, Combat, Upkeep.
  • Creator Slot — The left-most creature slot with special rules.
  • Exile — Cards removed from play; personal exile is unique to each player.
  • Glitch — A random Portal effect triggered starting Surge 4.
  • Token — A temporary creature created by another card.
  • Planeswalker Zone — The area where a player's planeswalker sits.
  • Artifact Zone — The area where a player's artifacts sit.
